PaybackChart — design system extension 09. Where the payback and the lifetime meet.

Q12. Replaces the unit-economics slide's 0–36 month bar ("the payback on a
bar with the 12-month tick"). Same axis, now with the money on it:

- a horizontal ink line: what one new customer cost (the CAC);
- a rising ink line: the margin it has brought back, month after month,
  until it leaves (its counted lifetime, capped at 36 months);
- **it pays back** where the rising line reaches the cost line: a dot; a
  bracket under the cost line measures the months of margin after;
- **it leaves first** (the loss): the line stops short; a bracket measures
  how short ("~€400 short") and a dashed thread (dashed: never reached)
  shows where it would have paid back had it stayed. The loss as money and
  as time in one picture — one finding, not two pieces of news;
- 12 months, "a commonly cited reference": a dotted line, labelled on the
  axis row. It situates, never judges (constraint 2);
- no margin: the rising line is the dashed, hatched "?" box with "missing:
  gross margin" (constraint 3); the cost line stays, it is known.

## Rules

Ink only, no red. Labels carry a paper halo where they cross a line.
Drawn at its real size, so its type never scales; `summary` says it in
words. `compact` (the hybrid's two columns): no cost label (the tile says
it), the time story on the axis row in one line, the reference explained in
the slide's note.
